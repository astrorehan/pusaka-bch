Pusaka in one minute

What happens to your BCH when you die? If nobody has your keys, the coins are lost for good. If somebody has them, they can take everything today.

Pusaka is a Bitcoin Cash wallet with the inheritance plan built in.

  1. Set it up once. Name your heirs and their shares (equal, your own split, or Islamic inheritance, faraid), three trusted contacts, a waiting time and a review period.
  2. Use it like any wallet. Send and receive, and swap on Chipnet (mainnet has no qualified pair yet). Every payment you send proves you are still here and restarts the waiting time. No reminders to press, unless you go a long time without paying anyone.
  3. If you go quiet, anyone can open a claim once the waiting time has passed, or two of your three contacts can open one sooner. The revised plans enforce the review period even when the owner pays the payout fee. Stop claim, or a payment you send, ends the claim and returns the plan to active use.
  4. After the review, anyone can press Pay the heirs. When the plan covers the network fee and every heir’s minimum payment, a visitor can submit the payout without a wallet.

The same plan covers a lost key: if the owner can no longer sign, two contacts can open a claim, and the coins reach the people named instead of being lost. A second wallet of the owner's own can be one of them.

No company holds the coins and no server stores the plan. Four SolidCash contracts do the work: the plan (PusakaAssetPlan) with its state in a CashTokens NFT, its receive address (PusakaV2Inbox), the review clock (PusakaV2ReviewAnchor, a BIP68 relative time lock) and a token vault (PusakaTokenVault) that keeps swapped tokens under the same rules.

The owner path: five minutes, with Paytaca or Cashonize

  1. Open Create a plan and connect your wallet with the QR code (WizardConnect).
  2. Add heirs and their shares, then three trusted contacts. Use ordinary receive addresses (bitcoincash:q…). Tip: use other receive addresses of your own wallet, and every payout comes back to you.
  3. Choose the waiting time and the review period (at least a day each), and a starting amount.
  4. Review and approve in your wallet. If your coins are spread over several addresses, press Prepare wallet first: a separate transaction that gathers them at your first receive address.
  5. Save the plan link when the app asks. Then try Send (the waiting time moves forward), Receive and Collect, I'm still here, Edit inheritance rules, and Take available BCH back from the ⋯ menu.

Every review sheet shows each output and fee before your wallet is asked to sign. The wallet signs only; the app broadcasts the exact transaction you reviewed and checks its ID.

The whole cycle yourself: about 30 minutes, on Chipnet

A plan you create on mainnet needs at least a day for each of the waiting time and the review, so that a short absence never costs an owner their savings. (The demo's examples were made by our script with a 1-minute wait.) To run every step yourself, use the quick test plan on Chipnet with test BCH from a Chipnet faucet:

  1. Create the plan as above, with Quick test ticked: a 1-minute waiting time and a 512-second review, the shortest the network can enforce.
  2. Wait for the waiting time to pass. The network clock moves with each block, so this takes a block or two.
  3. Press Open the claim on your plan. The review starts once the claim is confirmed in a block.
  4. When the review ends, press Pay the heirs. One transaction pays every heir.

Expect about 20 to 30 minutes in all, depending on how quickly blocks arrive.

What the network refuses

Each row is checked by a test that builds the attacker's transaction and expects it to fail, under the BCH 2026 limits and a strict BIP68 verifier (contract/test/v2/).

Someone tries to Result Test
Pay out one second before the review ends Refused: the anchor is not old enough rejects backdated openClaim locktime, unconfirmed anchor, insufficient MTP shift, and elapsed = delay - 1s
Pay out early with the owner paying the fee on the revised anchor Refused until BIP68 review matures keeps owner cancellation available and applies the same review time to an owner-sponsored payout
Use owner cancellation to produce a paid, stale, or still-approved plan Refused by the revised anchor accepts cancellation only into the next ACTIVE round with cleared approvals
Open a claim with a backdated time, or skip the review with a wrong sequence Refused rejects non-version-2 tx, disabled/height-mode/too-short sequence, and open+finalize in same tx
Swap an heir's address, or use a fake or stale claim marker Refused rejects substituted, plain-BCH, wrong-plan, wrong-round anchors, repeated openClaim, and hijacked payout
Vote twice as the same contact, replay a vote, forge the policy, or skim the receive address Refused rejects all adversarial attack vectors (fee-reserve siphon, contact double-vote/replay, substitution, policy forgery, CLTV bypass, inbox skim)
Stop a claim without being the owner Refused; the owner's stop works and restarts the clock enforces owner cancelClaim (rejects stranger, stops old finalize after delay, restarts fresh round 2 delay, allows ownerWithdraw)
Take part of the fee reserve as change while paying out Refused rejects a caller taking part of the review fee reserve as a self-funded change output
Redirect a payment, forge the state NFT or overcharge a collection Refused rejects adversarial attacks: recipient substitution, container dust violation, backdated locktime regression, forged NFTs, inbox skimming, excessive self-sweep fee, double container merge, and token junk
Swap through a fake pool, or with an expired quote Refused rejects expired quotes, fake pool scripts, zero minimum, wrong network metadata and old BCH-only plans

And what must work: a review that both a contact quorum and inactivity must respect, ten heirs at once, money that arrives after a payout or after the owner left still reaching the heirs, and a fresh browser restoring a plan from its link. Run them yourself (Node 24):

cd contract && npm install && npm test     # all contract tests, v1 and v2
cd app && npm install && npm run build && npm run test:ui

Current limits

  • Earlier plan addresses. The app still opens plans created with the earlier review anchor and labels them. Those contracts allow an early payout signed and sponsored by the owner to the registered heirs. The revised anchor enforces the delay for owner-sponsored payouts on newly created assets-2 plans.
  • Token inheritance after taking BCH back. Token payouts follow a completed BCH payout. If the owner takes back the available BCH and leaves tokens in the vault, the plan needs a BCH top-up before the heirs can claim them.

SolidCash implementation

The wallet integration led to an SDK signing report and a patch for funded split and merge. The upstream issue includes the stateful signing reproduction. The pull request covers the remaining split and merge paths with regression tests. Issue #3 · Pull request #7

If something doesn't work

Problem What to do
An input was spent during review Press Refresh transaction review to prepare from the current state. In the demo, you can also open the next example
The broadcast reply was lost during setup or a rules edit Open Recover a plan in the wallet and press Check submission. The app keeps the recovery file while the network status is uncertain
"The network clock has not reached this step yet." The network's clock runs behind real time. Wait a few minutes and press Refresh
"Could not reach the Bitcoin Cash network." The app could not reach a public BCH server. Press Refresh
A Testnet badge at the top You are in the Chipnet app. The mainnet app is at the site root
The wallet does not connect Use Paytaca, or Cashonize 0.9.0 or later, on the same network as the app, and scan the QR code
"Cancelled in your wallet. Nothing was sent." You declined in the wallet. Press the button again
Collect asks your wallet to approve A single small payment cannot pay its own fee under the contract's cap, so your wallet pays it and the plan keeps every satoshi received

Good to know

  • Keep the plan link. Every claim and payout needs the plan's rules, which travel in the link after the # and never reach a server. Pusaka keeps no copy. The BCH stays safe in the contracts without the link, but nobody can act on it.
  • Fees. Opening a claim and paying the heirs each cost about 4,000 to 5,000 satoshis, taken from the plan. Owner actions cost a similar fee from the owner's wallet or the plan. Pusaka takes no fee of its own today.
  • The network clock runs about an hour behind. Plans follow the median time of the last 11 blocks, so every date in the app says "about".
  • Up to 10 heirs, each with an ordinary BCH address, and each share at least 546 satoshis when paid.
  • Faraid is worked out in the browser. The contracts enforce the resulting numbers, not the rules of faraid.
  • It is not a legal will. It moves only the BCH and tokens held by the plan.
