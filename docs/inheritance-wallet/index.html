Architecture

BCH inheritance wallet architecture

How the wallet protects funds, receives payments, and handles claims.

17 November 2026. Original architecture selection and proof requirements. The resulting v2 implementation is described in the plan format and the verification record. The candidate constructions below preserve the reasoning and do not replace that current evidence. Product requirements are in PRODUCT.md. The current app and asset release are recorded here; the constructions below retain the original design investigation.

Constraints and existing evidence

  • The existing SolidCash contract has a shared deadline, fixed multi-heir payout and owner authorization through a co-spent ordinary P2PKH coin.
  • Paytaca mobile approved a mainnet plan creation through the current WizardConnect bridge. The offline relay serialization tests pass. A complete new wallet flow has not been tested in Paytaca; creation is not proof of every signing capability.
  • @wizardconnect/dapp in this app exposes transaction signing, not a portable arbitrary-message signing method. The protocol's optional extension mechanism does not imply Paytaca implements a proposed check-in or contact-signature API.
  • The SDK uses state NFTs and separate balance containers. Its deployed target address and logical BCH balance are not interchangeable. A "Receive" button cannot simply expose getAddress() and assume arbitrary deposits become balance.
  • Normal Paytaca coins have fixed P2PKH spending conditions. No monitor, pre-signed timer or xpub access changes their authorization rules. Avoid stale pre-signed transaction schemes that fail when the owner spends their inputs.

Candidate construction

Use an inheritance-aware BCH balance controlled by a single authenticated plan state. Keep Paytaca as the owner key holder; Pusaka builds the transactions and never requests a mnemonic or stores an owner spending key.

  1. A unique plan identity authenticates the current policy and state. Balance containers and any receive/inbox covenant must require that identity, the correct successor state and conservation of value. A matching token category alone is insufficient if unrelated NFTs or extra minting authority can forge the expected state role.
  2. send(destination, amount) requires fresh owner authorization. It reduces the logical balance by the approved payment and fees, preserves protected change and updates owner activity in the same transaction. It must work repeatedly.
  3. checkIn() is the small standalone owner transaction. Both it and send clear a pending claim and advance a round identifier. Require timestamp-based locktime, a non-final sequence and a non-decreasing deadline when using the existing absolute-deadline design; the caller-supplied locktime is not a trusted wall clock. The client uses fresh network time; stale authorization must not become a reusable future cancellation.
  4. A receive covenant or inbox accepts ordinary payments and binds them to this plan. The selection must prove independent incoming UTXOs, protected change, multiple deposits, dust, fresh-session discovery and post-claim receipts. If sweeping is necessary, it must not depend on a dead owner's signature. Do not assume an SDK method named deposit solves arbitrary incoming payments.
  5. Each contact has a configured identity. A transaction authorized by a contact records one approval for this plan, policy version and activity round. Distinct contacts count once. The quorum creates a Review state. An expired inactivity condition may also create Review without contact cooperation.
  6. Only after the complete review delay may an unrestricted submitter request the predetermined payout. Owner cancellation spends the same authenticated claim state, so its invalidation is enforced by consensus rather than an app flag.

Keep the current Pusaka.scash and existing plan links readable as v1. Build v2 as a separate artifact and explicit plan version. Do not reuse the old artifact for a new policy or imply funded contracts are upgradeable.

Two immediate engineering gates

A. Prove the wallet benefit before changing the screens

Start with a minimal partial-payment contract, the real SDK and the current external signer. Exercise: fund, pay part, retain change, receive again, pay again, and recover the plan from a new session.

The present owner-co-spend pattern is a candidate authorization path because it uses ordinary wallet signing. Prove the required owner input and its change, including the case where nearly all owner funds are in Pusaka. Do not require a surprising second top-up before each payment. A small persistent owner coin or a different supported authorization route must have explicit fee accounting.

Gate A passes only when funds remain covered after partial spending, new receipts have an enforceable claim path, and the real Paytaca request is understandable. A simulator-only send is useful evidence but does not pass the wallet gate.

B. Anchor the review delay to the actual claim transition

Do not implement reviewEndsAt = tx.locktime + reviewPeriod as the only protection. A hostile claimant can choose an old locktime, allowing a newly opened review to appear already finished. tx.time constrains the spending transaction's locktime; it does not supply an incorruptible "now" to store in state.

Preferred candidate: create a dedicated authenticated claim UTXO on entry to Review. Finalization must consume it with a consensus-enforced relative time lock (OP_CHECKSEQUENCEVERIFY, transaction version 2, time-based BIP68 encoding). The delay starts from that newly created output's chain age. Round the configured duration upward to 512-second units and explain network-time approximation.

Require the specific claim anchor and reject substitutes. Owner cancellation consumes or invalidates it. Unauthenticated deposits and repeated votes must not recreate it, restart its age or advance the round. If the normal SDK state update always recreates the relevant UTXO, a separate claim covenant is necessary. Incoming funds during Review must either join without changing the anchor or remain in a safe inbox claimable by the same heirs; they must not need owner consent to join.

The compiler, SDK input sequence handling, output identity and full transaction limits need an executable proof. The CashScript documentation currently notes a time-based sequence limitation in its transaction builder. Do not assume that language support alone proves the installed SolidCash path can build it. If it cannot, investigate a small dedicated covenant and lower-level builder; do not replace a seconds-based duration with a block count without revisiting semantics.

Authorization and convenience options

Contact co-spend transactions can use ordinary wallet approvals, like the current owner path, subject to proving the contact is the authorized input in the relevant method. There is no need to invent a portable message API for the first version.

checkDataSig makes signed statements a possible later construction on BCH. It does not make EIP-712 or WebAuthn signatures drop-in replacements. Prove the exact digest, signature format and wallet capability. Bind any signed message to the network, unique plan, policy version, action, activity round or claim identity and freshness condition. Reject a pre-claim message used to cancel a later claim.

Relayers can pay fees and submit authorized actions. They must have no authority to redirect funds, fabricate life proofs or be required for the owner to stop a claim. Notifications and indexing may use a service, with direct chain access and portable plan data as fallback. "No server" is not a reason to remove useful UX.

Passkey support needs a separate signature-verification and key-recovery design. Browser biometrics alone are not a BCH-verifiable owner authorization. Keep it outside the initial wallet proof rather than presenting a server decision as equivalent to the earlier on-chain passkey verification.

Assets and trading

The first wallet proof is BCH. A trading demonstration additionally needs one named adapter and fungible CashToken containers whose balances can be distributed under the same family policy. Token category, returned amount, protected change, slippage limits, fee payer and recipient must be bound into the approved transaction.

An asset sitting in a pool, an order, a lending position or a custodial exchange is not automatically in the inheritable balance. Supporting positions requires a proved redemption/claim adapter, not just displaying their market value. Nonfungible assets require an explicit recipient rule; a percentage split cannot divide an NFT.

Required adversarial checks

  • Stranger cannot send, check in, change the policy or cancel a claim.
  • Wrong or fabricated plan NFT cannot authorize balance spending or a receive sweep.
  • Payee substitution, excessive fees and unprotected change are rejected.
  • Incoming funds, repeated votes and self-transfers without owner authorization cannot renew life or indefinitely delay a valid claim.
  • Same contact twice, another plan's approval and a previous round's approval do not satisfy the quorum.
  • Backdated opening locktime, same-transaction opening/finalization, wrong anchor, disabled/wrong-unit sequence and early finalization cannot bypass the review delay.
  • An owner action invalidates the old anchor and all old approvals; an old message cannot stop a later claim.
  • Concurrent owner and payout submissions resolve through the authenticated state; neither UI assumes success before chain confirmation.
  • Every heir gets the configured share of the latest covered balance; rounding, token amounts, carrier values, dust and fee reserves are accounted for.
  • Empty balances, deposits during Review and deposits after a payout have defined, recoverable spending paths. No old receive address strands future funds.
  • Lost relay session and a fresh browser can recover the same plan using public metadata and the owner's wallet, without storing a private spending key.

Sources

  • BCH model and covenants: spending conditions, introspection and constrained change outputs.

  • WizardConnect protocol and extensions: transaction signing is the base interaction; extra capabilities require wallet support.
